The ordinances targeted individuals considered outside the estates-based social order, including itinerant soldiers and beggars. They were regarded as potentially disruptive to security and public order and therefore subject to strict regulation.

The Würzburg court implemented Circle-wide decisions through its own decrees. In 1714, cooperation among the leading Imperial Circles was emphasised in order to restrict unauthorised mobility and safeguard public peace.

The position of Jewish communities within the Empire was legally ambivalent. On the one hand, protective charters and imperial or territorial privileges granted a degree of security and allowed for economic activity. On the other hand, they were subject to numerous restrictions, special taxes, and local threats of expulsion. Within broader police ordinances, they were at times regulated alongside other mobile or non-integrated groups, although their legal status was formally based on protection and tolerated residence.
