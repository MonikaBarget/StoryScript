You meet Maria Theresa, Archduchess of Austria and Queen of Hungary and Bohemia. The early days of her reign were shaped by the War of the Austrian Succession (1740-1748).

I have nothing further to say on this matter.

I am Maria Theresa. In times of war, much depends on maintaining order between the territories. What would you like to know?

What role did the Imperial Circles play in the war? You mention an agreement with the Franconian Circle concerning deserters. What exactly was that? Why was it necessary to establish such arrangements at all?

The Imperial Circles were important mediating and organisational structures in this period. They assisted not only with provisioning and recruitment, but could also act as partners in military agreements when shared security interests were at stake.

Understood.

In 1745 I concluded an agreement with the Franconian Imperial Circle that can be described as a “cartel.” It stipulated that deserters and other military offenders who crossed territorial borders were to be mutually returned. The Franconian Circle committed itself to handing over persons who had fled from my troops or violated military regulations to my authorities—and the same applied in the opposite direction.

Thank you, I have another question.

In a war such as the War of the Austrian Succession, it was easy for soldiers to change sides or simply desert. Because the Imperial Circles and territories lay in close proximity to one another, no ruler could rely on borders alone. Such agreements were intended to maintain military discipline and to prevent individual territories from being weakened by the movement of deserters.
