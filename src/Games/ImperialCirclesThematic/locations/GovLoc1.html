The so-called morose Stände were estates within an imperical circle who failed to pay their contributions, leading to tensions. The Ius Collectarum granted circles the privilege to collect certain taxes. They each kept a collective fund (Crays-Cassa) allocated for common causes and for supporting individual estates in need. Payments could be made in cash or natural resources (e.g., gunpowder, grain). Moser (p. 49) notes that while cash was preferred, alternatives were sometimes permitted.
