Maria Theresa (1717–1780) was the only female ruler of the Habsburg dominions and a central figure in 18th-century European politics. She implemented wide-ranging administrative, fiscal, and military reforms that strengthened state capacity, especially after the setbacks of the War of the Austrian Succession. Her reign is often associated with “enlightened absolutism,” although recent scholarship stresses the pragmatic and dynastic nature of her reforms rather than purely ideological Enlightenment influence.
