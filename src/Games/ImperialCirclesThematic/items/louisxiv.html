The reign of Louis XIV (1643–1715) is often associated with the consolidation of absolutist monarchy in France. His court at Palace of Versailles functioned as both a political and cultural center, reinforcing royal authority through ritual and patronage. Fiscal pressures from continuous warfare and court expenditure, however, strained the monarchy’s long-term stability. Current historiography nuances the notion of “absolutism” and points to the continued importance of negotiation with elites and s.
