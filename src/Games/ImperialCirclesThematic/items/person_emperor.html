The Holy Roman Emperor was an elected ruler, chosen by a small group of prince electors. This process was first formalised in the Golden Bull of 1356. , The office carried symbolic claims to universal Christian authority in the footsteps of Charlemagne, but its actual power depended heavily on negotiation with territorial princes. Over time, imperial influence varied significantly depending on dynastic resources. Current historiography stresses the emperor’s role as a mediator within a complex legal order.
