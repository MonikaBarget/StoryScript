Gravamina, the plural of Gravamen, are complaints voiced as the basis of a lawsuit. The term derives from the Latin verb gravare, which means "to weigh down", and was common in early modern Europe. In the Holy Roman Empire, the expression is often found in relation to the rights of estates to formally influence princely bureaucracies, especially at the territorial diets (provincial assembly of estates). The imperial circles, too, handled gravamina submitted by their members.
