The Holy Roman Empire (c. 800–1806) was a multi-layered political entity in Central Europe, comprising hundreds of semi-autonomous territories under an imperial framework. Its structure combined feudal hierarchies with elective monarchy and collaborative bodies such as the Imperial Diet(s) and imperical circles. It is not a “state” in the sense of modern nations but a legal and constitutional order that resembles transnational organisations like the European Union today. Recent scholarship emphasises its durability and adaptability rather than its supposed political weakness.
